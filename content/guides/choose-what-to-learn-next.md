"Learn more tools" is difficult to finish. "Find missing and repeated IDs in a small practice list, then explain the checks" gives you something specific to try and review.

Use this guide to choose a next learning step, not to rate your suitability for a job. No course purchase, account, or personal information is needed for the exercise below. Keep any practice files in your own workspace; there is nothing to upload here.

## Start With a Task, Not a Tool List

The National Careers Service suggests looking at job descriptions and reflecting on work, education, and everyday experience when identifying skills to develop. [Understand and develop your skills](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/identifying-skills-and-upskilling).

Choose one responsibility you want to understand better. Keep an employer's stated requirements separate from your learning plan; completing a practice task does not establish that you meet them. [Read a Job Posting](/guides/read-a-job-posting/) helps make that distinction.

Write three short lines in your own notes:

- **Work to understand:** the specific task, not a whole occupation.
- **Evidence I already have:** something I have actually done and can explain.
- **Next uncertainty:** a step I cannot yet perform or check confidently.

You may need clearer evidence or an answer about the role rather than another course. If the work itself is unclear, start with [an informational conversation](/guides/plan-an-informational-conversation/) or the employer's public materials.

## Define a Small Practice Result

Try this Jobplorer planning outline before collecting tutorials:

1. **Task:** one action you will attempt.
2. **Input:** invented or clearly reusable material, not customer or employer records.
3. **Output:** something you can inspect, such as a short explanation or a marked-up example.
4. **Check:** what would show that the attempt did what you intended?
5. **Review point:** a time you can realistically set aside to inspect the result, even if it is unfinished.

For a fictional record-checking task, the output could be a list of row numbers and reasons to investigate. There is no need to build an entire application or dashboard first. If the task is too large, reduce its input or limit it to one check.

## Choose a Resource for the Gap

The National Careers Service recommends checking a course's time commitment, costs, study format, support, and account requirements before enrolling. [Get the most out of online learning](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/getting-the-most-out-of-online-learning).

Look for material that explains the step you want to practise at your current level. For a named tool, start with its official documentation and check that the instructions fit the version you use. Do not assume that a whole course is needed for one unanswered question, or that a course completion proves broad competence.

For the example below, a first pass can be done on paper. A later attempt could use a spreadsheet or a small script you already know how to run. No specific product, subscription, or credential is recommended.

## Try a Fictional Record-Checking Exercise

All six records are invented. They are not employee, applicant, or customer data. Each numbered item represents one row:

1. **DEMO-101:** Setup note
2. **DEMO-102:** FAQ update
3. **DEMO-102:** FAQ update
4. **(blank):** Link check
5. **DEMO-103:** Status note
6. **DEMO-104:** Search note

For this exercise only, an ID should be present and should occur once. The label **(blank)** represents a missing value, not an actual ID. These are the exercise's rules, not assumptions to impose on a real dataset.

**Your task:** identify every row with a missing or repeated ID and give the reason. Preserve the original six rows. Do not invent a replacement ID or delete a repeated row to make the result look clean.

Try the check before reading the worked result. If you use software, compare its flags with a manual pass rather than trusting a successful run alone.

## Compare With the Worked Result

- **Rows 2 and 3:** both use `DEMO-102`, so both need a repeated-ID flag under the exercise rules.
- **Row 4:** needs a missing-ID flag.
- **Rows 1, 5, and 6:** pass these two checks only. That does not establish that every part of those records is correct.

Flagging a record is different from deciding how to repair it. The example does not tell you why the duplicate exists or what the missing ID should be.

For a second attempt, make a separate practice copy and change row 3 to `DEMO-105`. The repeated-ID flags should disappear, while row 4 should still be flagged as missing. Keep the original example so you can compare the two runs. This checks a small change in input, not reliability on every possible dataset.

## Review, Repeat, or Reduce

At your review point, use four questions:

- What could I complete without following each example step?
- Which check failed or still needs an explanation?
- What did I look up, and can I now explain why it helped?
- What is the smallest useful next attempt?

If the result is unclear, repeat a smaller version or ask a specific question. If the checks work and you can explain them, try a modest variation before adding another tool. If the task no longer relates to the work you want to explore, revise the plan instead of treating unfinished practice as a personal score.

Bring one finding and one next action to your [weekly job search review](/job-seeker-tools/#your-weekly-job-search-review). Keep the learning plan small enough to revisit alongside your other priorities.

## Describe Practice Honestly

After completing your own attempt, describe what you actually did, which input you used, and what remains limited. An appropriate description might be "checked six fictional records for missing and repeated IDs" only if you performed those checks yourself.

Do not turn a study exercise into employment history, claim an unmeasured improvement, or label yourself an expert after one task. The [project case study guide](/guides/build-a-project-case-study/) helps explain a contribution and its evidence.
