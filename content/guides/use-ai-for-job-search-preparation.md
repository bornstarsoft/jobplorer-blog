An AI suggestion is something to check, not evidence of a skill or a result. A useful preparation exercise leaves you with clearer questions or clearer wording, without creating a different version of your experience.

This guide uses invented material for practice. It does not recommend a product, provide an AI service, or predict an application outcome. For the employer side of the topic, read [AI in Recruitment](/posts/the-rise-of-ai-in-recruitment/).

## Check the Rules for the Stage You Are In

Read the employer's current instructions for application writing, take-home tasks, tests, and interviews separately. Do not assume permission for one activity covers another. If the instructions are unclear, ask through the verified contact before using AI for assessed work.

For a concrete example, the UK Civil Service distinguishes preparation and writing support from uses it does not permit, including invented experience and AI-produced responses during interviews. That is guidance for its own recruitment process, not a rule for every employer. [Civil Service Careers: Artificial intelligence and recruitment](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/artificial-intelligence-and-recruitment/).

Do not use these exercises to bypass an assessment instruction or present generated answers as unaided work.

## Practice Without Personal Information

The University of Edinburgh Careers Service warns about sharing personal data and about inaccurate or invented AI output. It recommends cross-checking research rather than treating AI as the only source. [Guidelines on the use of Generative AI in the application process](https://careers.ed.ac.uk/cvs-and-applications/guidelines-on-the-use-of-generative-ai-in-the-application-process).

Start with the fictional material below. It does not require a resume upload, employer documents, account credentials, contact details, or real client information. For any separate tool you choose to use, check its current data-handling information. A request in a prompt is not a privacy guarantee, and deleting a name does not establish that a document is anonymous.

You can also do every comparison in this guide yourself without using an AI tool.

For a short revisit, [try two rewrite reviews](#two-short-rewrite-reviews): keep a preference from becoming a requirement, then keep a planned check separate from an observed result.

## A Fictional Practice Pack

Both items below are invented. They are not a real vacancy or a reader's work history.

**Posting excerpt:** "Help maintain support notes, test setup instructions, and share clear updates. Familiarity with spreadsheets is helpful."

**Experience note:** "For a study project, I wrote an installation checklist. A teammate tried it and found a missing setup step. I added that step. My teammate built the application. We did not measure support requests."

Keep these two inputs separate. The posting describes work to investigate; it does not add skills or achievements to the experience note.

## Three Small Prompts to Try

These are Jobplorer editorial exercises, not tested product benchmarks. Include the relevant fictional input with the prompt. The instructions may still be ignored or misunderstood, so check the result yourself.

### Separate What the Posting Says

> Using only the fictional posting excerpt, list responsibilities, stated preferences, and unanswered questions separately. For each item, show the words in the excerpt that support it. If the excerpt does not answer something, label it unknown. Do not invent requirements or decide whether an applicant qualifies.

**Check:** spreadsheet familiarity should remain a preference in this example. The excerpt supplies no required years of experience, location, or work arrangement. Use [Read a Job Posting](/guides/read-a-job-posting/) for the same exercise without AI.

### Clarify Without Expanding the Claim

> Using only the fictional experience note, suggest one clearer sentence about the applicant's contribution. Preserve the study-project context and the teammate's contribution. Do not add leadership, tools, credentials, numbers, or outcomes. List the facts used and anything the note leaves unknown.

**Check:** compare each proposed action and result against the input. A smoother sentence is not acceptable if it changes who did the work.

### Generate Questions, Not Personal Answers

> Using the fictional posting and experience note, suggest two practice questions about documenting and checking setup steps. Do not write answers for the applicant or claim these are the employer's actual questions. Label the questions as practice material.

**Check:** practice your own answer from truthful experience. Do not memorize an invented story or use the exercise as live interview assistance. [Prepare for an Interview](/guides/prepare-for-an-interview/) provides a separate preparation path.

## Spot the Unsupported Additions

Here is an intentionally flawed, invented rewrite, not output from a tested AI product:

> Led a documentation project and reduced support requests by 40%.

The practice note does not establish leadership or any measured reduction. It describes a study exercise and a revision after a teammate's check. Remove the unsupported additions rather than asking for a more impressive version.

A fact-preserving alternative for this fictional note is:

> Wrote a study-project installation checklist and added a missing setup step after a teammate tested it.

This sentence is supported by the invented input, not a claim anyone should copy as their own experience. The [project case study guide](/guides/build-a-project-case-study/) explains how to connect a real contribution to evidence and limits.

## Review Before Using Anything

- Can you support every action, skill, date, and result from your own records?
- Did a preference become a requirement, or an unknown become a confident answer?
- Did the wording change a team achievement into an individual claim?
- Have you opened and checked any source the output cites?
- Does the intended use follow the current employer instructions for this stage?

Keep your original draft and review changes in your own document. Do not replace a truthful sentence with one you cannot explain. Use the [cover letter guide](/guides/write-a-focused-cover-letter/) or [resume checklist](/posts/why-87-percent-of-job-seekers-abandon-their-resumes/) before submitting materials.
