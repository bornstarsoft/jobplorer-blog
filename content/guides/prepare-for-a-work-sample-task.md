Before making a polished answer, establish what you have been asked to produce. A useful preparation habit is to separate the supplied facts, the requested output, and the questions that remain open.

This guide is for preparation, not completing a live assessment for someone else. The exercise is fictional and needs no account, personal information, upload, or particular software. Follow the employer's actual instructions; this is not a scoring rubric or a promise of an outcome.

## Read the Brief Before Starting

The National Careers Service advises reading instructions carefully when preparing for an assessment centre or presentation. Use the actual invitation to identify the format rather than assuming every employer uses the same process. [National Careers Service: Interview tips](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice).

In your own notes, identify:

- **Deliverable:** a document, presentation, code sample, or another specified output.
- **Audience and purpose:** who will read or use it, and what question it should answer.
- **Inputs:** the supplied files, facts, and examples you may use.
- **Boundaries:** length, format, deadline and time zone, and any stated working-time limit.
- **Permitted help:** tools, references, collaboration, or AI use explicitly allowed by the instructions.
- **Submission:** the destination, file naming, and contact for technical problems.

A submission deadline is not necessarily a statement of expected effort. If the expected scope or effort is unclear, ask before committing to the task rather than silently expanding it. For an unexpected invitation, [check the recruiter and employer](/guides/check-job-offers-and-recruiters/) before opening files or sharing details.

## Clarify Gaps Without Inventing Answers

Where questions are allowed, ask about the specific missing detail: "Should the output be a one-page summary or a slide deck?" is easier to answer than "What do you want?"

The University of Edinburgh's assessment-centre guidance describes case-study exercises where candidates explain their reasoning and assumptions. It also notes that some briefs do not allow additional interaction. Check the rules for your own task. [University of Edinburgh: Assessment centres](https://careers.ed.ac.uk/interviews-and-assessment-centres/assessment-centres).

If clarification is unavailable, distinguish an assumption from a supplied fact and state a limitation where the format permits. Do not invent customer information, product features, results, or permission to use external help. For a restricted task, practise separately with fictional material rather than uploading the employer's brief to an AI service.

## Try a Fictional Brief

Everything in this exercise is invented, including the demo workspace. It is not a real employer test or a product tutorial. You can work through it on paper.

> Rewrite the draft below for a first-time demo user. Use only the supplied facts. Produce three numbered steps, one reset caution, and one question about missing information. Do not invent a sign-in method or a save feature.

### Given Facts

1. Open the demo workspace.
2. Inside the workspace, choose Examples.
3. In Examples, choose Try sample.
4. Reset removes changes made in the demo.
5. The supplied information does not describe a save option.

### Draft to Check

> Sign in with your work email. Open Examples and choose Try sample. Your changes save automatically, and Reset keeps your edits.

Before reading the possible response, mark each statement as supported, contradicted, or not supplied. Then check whether your rewrite meets the requested format.

### One Possible Response

1. Open the demo workspace.
2. Choose Examples.
3. Choose Try sample.

**Reset caution:** Reset removes changes made in the demo.

**Question:** Can sample changes be saved before using Reset?

The email sign-in and automatic-saving claims are not supplied; remove them rather than substituting another imagined feature. The claim that Reset keeps edits contradicts the facts. The question identifies missing information without asserting that a save option exists.

This is one response that follows the fictional brief, not a model answer for every employer. It does not establish proficiency in a real product or occupation.

## Check Coverage Before Polishing

Compare the output with the brief, not with an imagined ideal submission. In the exercise, three steps plus a caution and a question are enough; a slide deck or working application would add unrequested scope.

- Does every requested element appear in the output?
- Can each factual claim be traced to an allowed input?
- Are unsupported assumptions removed or clearly identified?
- Does the format match the instructions?
- Have you left time to open and inspect the actual file you will submit?

If you cannot complete something within the stated constraints, describe what is unfinished where allowed. Do not present an untested feature or a partial result as complete.

## Explain the Choices You Actually Made

Prepare to describe one decision, its supporting evidence, and a remaining limitation. For this exercise: "I removed the saving claim because the brief does not describe saving. I kept the question separate so it cannot be mistaken for an instruction."

That explanation is about the supplied material, not a claim that all assessors prefer the same answer. For discussion practice, use [Prepare for an Interview](/guides/prepare-for-an-interview/). If you are presenting an existing project rather than responding to a new brief, use [Build a Project Case Study](/guides/build-a-project-case-study/).

## Submit Through the Agreed Route

Check the destination against the original invitation. Open the final attachment or permitted preview, confirm it is the intended version, and follow any instructions for sources or tool disclosure. Do not substitute a public repository or public sharing link for a private submission unless instructed.

Do not publish an employer's task or your response as a portfolio piece without permission. Use a separate fictional exercise when permission is uncertain. Keep any confirmation privately and follow the stated next steps; see [Follow Up After an Interview](/guides/follow-up-after-an-interview/) for cautious communication prompts.

At your [weekly job search review](/job-seeker-tools/#your-weekly-job-search-review), note one preparation habit to repeat and one uncertainty to clarify next time. There is no need to store assessment material in Jobplorer.
