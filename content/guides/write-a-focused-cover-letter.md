A cover letter can connect the work described in a posting with an example you can explain. It does not need to retell every line of your resume or promise that you can do everything.

Start with the employer's current submission instructions. A letter is not required for every application, and this guide does not predict selection or an interview.

## Check the Requested Format First

- **A letter is requested:** note the length, file type, destination, and any questions to answer.
- **The form has a supporting-statement question:** answer that question directly; do not assume a separate letter is a substitute.
- **The instructions say not to attach one:** follow that instruction.
- **The instructions are unclear:** check the official application page or verified contact rather than inventing a submission rule.

These are reading prompts, not rules shared by all employers. Use [Read a Job Posting](/guides/read-a-job-posting/) to separate stated criteria from assumptions.

## Choose One Connection to Explain

The National Careers Service recommends tailoring a letter to the role and supporting statements with facts. Its [cover letter guidance](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/covering-letter) is a useful preparation reference, not a universal requirement to submit a letter.

In your own document, put three things next to each other:

1. **The work:** one responsibility copied accurately into your private research notes.
2. **The example:** something you actually did, including your contribution.
3. **The connection:** why that example helps explain your interest in this work, without claiming identical experience.

If you need a clearer example, first [build a project case study](/guides/build-a-project-case-study/). The National Careers Service's [application form advice](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/application-forms) also describes using relevant examples from activities or volunteering, not only paid employment.

## An Annotated Fictional Letter

Imagine an invented Documentation Assistant posting that requests a short letter and mentions explaining setup steps. The applicant and experience below are fictional. This is a practice example, not a genuine vacancy or text to present as your own experience.

> Dear Recruitment Team,
>
> I am applying for the Documentation Assistant role, reference DOC-EXAMPLE. The posting's focus on clear setup instructions interests me because I enjoyed documenting a small study project.
>
> For that project, I wrote a setup guide for a puzzle prototype. I recorded the steps from a clean installation and asked a teammate to try them without my help. Their questions revealed that I had skipped an environment setting, which I then added. I wrote the guide; my teammate built the prototype. This was a study exercise, not a customer deployment.
>
> I would like to develop that careful approach to explaining steps in a documentation role. My resume includes the project context. Thank you for considering my application.
>
> Kind regards,
> Example Applicant

Notice what each part does:

- **Opening:** identifies the role and a concrete responsibility instead of praising an unknown company culture.
- **Middle:** describes an action, a check, and a revision. It separates individual and team work without inventing a metric.
- **Closing:** explains the connection without claiming professional experience the applicant does not have.

The greeting and structure are editorial examples, not required wording. Use the actual recipient details and requested format. Do not guess a person's name or title.

## Replace Claims With Evidence

**Vague:** "I am the perfect candidate and an excellent communicator."

**More specific, if true:** "I revised the setup instructions after a teammate identified a missing step."

The second sentence gives the reader something to ask about. It does not prove suitability on its own. Remove a claim you cannot support instead of adding stronger adjectives or invented outcomes.

## Before Sending

Check the employer, role reference, requested format, and every factual example. Remove placeholders such as DOC-EXAMPLE and Example Applicant. Make sure the letter and resume describe the same dates and responsibilities.

Open the final attachment, or preview the submitted text if the portal permits it. Keep your own private copy. Do not add identity documents, private client material, or another person's contact details to this practice example. Prepare and save your actual letter in a document you control, not in a public comment or shared example.

Use the [resume checklist](/posts/why-87-percent-of-job-seekers-abandon-their-resumes/) for a final materials review. Be ready to discuss any example in your letter using [Prepare for an Interview](/guides/prepare-for-an-interview/).
