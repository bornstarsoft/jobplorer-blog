## Your Weekly Job Search Review

[Choose a short practice route](/job-seeker-tools/guide-finder/#practice-routes) for reading postings, explaining experience, preparing questions or checking a task brief. Each connects three fictional exercises with a specific next-visit task.

1. Choose a role family and location to focus on this week.
2. [Recheck a saved posting](/guides/read-a-job-posting/#recheck-a-saved-posting). Note what changed, what the employer confirmed, and what remains unresolved.
3. Compare the responsibilities with examples from your work, study, or projects.
4. Select the applications you can prepare carefully. Record a next action and date for each.
5. [Choose the next action for each open application](#choose-the-next-application-action) before changing your search terms.

Bring one search note from last week. [Compare two search visits](/guides/refine-your-job-search-terms/#compare-two-search-visits) to decide whether to keep a query, test one change, or verify a detail before searching again.

Start with [job site comparisons](/job-sites/), a [career guide](/career-guides/), or a [country guide](/country-guides/).

If your next step is learning rather than another application, [Choose What to Learn Next](/guides/choose-what-to-learn-next/) helps define a small practice task and check its result without using personal records.

### Choose the Next Application Action

Use the last status you could verify for each role. If a verified request still needs a response, handle that branch first rather than marking the application as waiting. Among requests, check any deadlines and time zones the employer actually gave.

- **Receipt unconfirmed:** You tried to submit but have no matching receipt. [Review application receipts](#confirm-application-receipt) in the employer's stated channel before sending again. A missing email alone does not establish failure.
- **Additional request:** The verified channel asks for something you have not sent. [Check the requested item, format and destination](#before-you-submit) before responding. If the request arrived unexpectedly, [verify the contact independently](/guides/check-job-offers-and-recruiters/) before sharing anything.
- **Waiting for a reply:** Receipt is confirmed and nothing is currently requested. Note any response window the employer gave. If it has not passed, keep it as your next checkpoint. If it passed or no timing was given, check the stated status channel at your next review. Do not read silence as a decision or assume a message is required.

**Three fictional review notes:**

- **DEMO-301:** Submission attempted, no matching receipt. Next action: check the application history for this reference.
- **DEMO-302:** The portal requests a readable replacement PDF. Next action: open the corrected file and compare it with the portal instructions before responding.
- **DEMO-204:** Receipt confirmed, no further request or timing given. Next action: check the portal at the next review and spend today's preparation time on another role.

For next time, keep one line per open role in your own notes: **last confirmed status / one next action / check date**. A check date is your own reminder, not an employer deadline. At the next review, start with notes whose check dates have arrived or whose verified instructions have changed.

If the employer has replied, [choose what to do with the reply](#act-on-an-application-reply) instead of leaving the role marked as waiting.

### Act on an Application Reply

Match the message to the role or reference and the employer's stated channel. An update such as "still reviewing" is not an invitation or a decision. If the request is unexpected or the sender is unclear, [verify the contact before replying](/guides/check-job-offers-and-recruiters/).

- **Interview invitation:** Check whether you need to pick or confirm a slot. Verify the date, time zone, format, and any requested preparation. Once the arrangements are clear, [prepare for the interview](/guides/prepare-for-an-interview/#start-with-the-posting).
- **Additional materials requested:** Note the exact item, format, destination, and any stated deadline. [Run the final file check](#before-you-submit) and send only what was verified and requested. If the instructions are incomplete, clarify the missing detail through the stated channel before sending.
- **Process closed for this application:** If the employer explicitly says your application will not progress, move it out of your active list. Record the outcome as stated. Do not guess why; carry one question or example you want to improve into another application.

**Fictional next review:** Last week, `DEMO-204` was waiting. This week, the verified portal asks you to choose an interview slot. Replace "check for a reply" with "choose a slot and confirm the time zone." Leave the interview marked as not booked until the employer's channel confirms it.

In your own notes, keep **what changed / next action / what to verify next time**. When you [return to the weekly review](#your-weekly-job-search-review), check whether that action is complete before choosing another. For a closed application, bring forward the preparation note, not another status check.

## Application Record Template

Keep one record per role in your own private notes or spreadsheet.

- Employer and role:
- Official posting URL and reference number:
- Location and work arrangement:
- Date checked and closing date, if listed:
- Responsibilities and evidence I can show:
- Documents requested and resume version used:
- Application destination and submission date:
- Confirmation received:
- Current status: researching / preparing / submitted / response received / closed
- Next action and date:

Avoid including passwords, identity documents, or confidential employer information in a shared tracker.

## Update Your Application Draft

When a recheck confirms the same role but finds different instructions, compare the employer's current request with the draft you already have. Keep material that still fits, prepare what is newly requested, and set aside anything that depends on an unanswered question.

In the fictional `DEMO-204` example, the employer still requests a resume and now asks for a short writing sample. The posting does not say whether an existing sample is acceptable or whether it should be a link or an attachment.

- **Keep:** The resume example about writing setup instructions still fits the role. Keep the description of what you actually did.
- **Prepare:** Identify one relevant sample you may share, such as instructions for a personal project. Check whether it fits once the requested scope and format are clear.
- **Verify before sending:** Find out whether an existing sample is allowed and how to submit it. If the employer provides a verified channel for questions, ask about those two details. Do not assume a public link is accepted.

In your own notes, leave one line for next time: **What is ready / what is unanswered / when I will check again.** For `DEMO-204`: resume ready; sample type and format unconfirmed; recheck the posting before sending. At your [next weekly review](#your-weekly-job-search-review), update that line if the employer clarifies the request. If instructions have changed again, [recheck the posting](/guides/read-a-job-posting/#recheck-a-saved-posting) before reusing the draft.

If the employer sends a defined task instead of asking for an existing sample, [check the work sample brief](/guides/prepare-for-a-work-sample-task/#read-the-brief-before-starting). Once the requested materials and submission destination are clear, use the [final submission checklist](#before-you-submit).

## Before You Submit

Check the files and fields you are about to send, not just the draft you worked on. If the instructions changed or you are still waiting for a detail, return to the [application draft review](#update-your-application-draft).

1. **Match the destination.** Compare the employer, role and reference with the current posting. Use the verified application route and recheck any stated deadline and time zone.
2. **Open the actual files.** Match each file to a requested item and format. Open the final copies, check that they are readable and for the right role, and remove comments or confidential material that should not be shared. Include links only if appropriate; check that the intended reader can open them without making private work public.
3. **Review what will be sent.** Compare automatically filled fields with your information. Check the selected filenames or attachments in the employer's preview if one is available. If a requested item, file version or destination is still unclear, resolve it before sending.

### Final Check: A Fictional Example

In the fictional `DEMO-204` example, the employer's current instructions now confirm that an existing writing sample is allowed as a PDF attachment. The preview shows the updated resume but an older sample draft. Replace that attachment with the intended PDF, open the final copy and check the selected filename again. A saved draft or a preview is not a submission confirmation.

### Pick Up at Your Next Review

In your own notes, keep one line for this role:

- **Not sent yet:** Record the remaining check and when you will return to it. For `DEMO-204`: replace and verify the sample PDF before sending.
- **Sent:** Note the date and final file versions. [Check whether it was received](#confirm-application-receipt) before treating the application as confirmed.

Bring that note to your [weekly job search review](#your-weekly-job-search-review). No files or notes need to be entered in Jobplorer.

If you need to compare responsibilities and preferences again, see [Read a Job Posting](/guides/read-a-job-posting/). For a specific document, use the [resume review checklist](/posts/why-87-percent-of-job-seekers-abandon-their-resumes/), [Build a Project Case Study](/guides/build-a-project-case-study/) for evidence of your contribution, or [Write a Focused Cover Letter](/guides/write-a-focused-cover-letter/) when a letter is requested.

## Confirm Application Receipt

Use the employer's stated confirmation channel, such as a receipt on the submission screen, an email, or the application portal. Match the confirmation to the role and reference if shown. A receipt confirms only what it says; it does not by itself confirm an interview or a decision.

- **Receipt confirmed:** Record the confirmation date and any next step the employer states. Leave the timing unknown if none is given.
- **Receipt not confirmed:** Note that you attempted to send the application. A missing email does not establish that it failed. Check the stated channel for the same role before sending again.
- **Action requested:** If the verified channel asks for a correction or missing item, [check the files and submission details](#before-you-submit) before responding. If a request arrives unexpectedly, [verify the contact](/guides/check-job-offers-and-recruiters/) before sharing anything.

### Carry One Note Into Next Week

In the fictional `DEMO-204` example, you attached the corrected PDF and pressed Submit. The screen closed without a receipt. In your own notes: **Submission attempted; receipt unconfirmed / check the stated portal at the next review.** At that review, the portal shows "Application received" for `DEMO-204` with no further instructions. Update the note to **Receipt confirmed / no next step stated** and use the review time for another application rather than resending this one.

For your own application, record **what the source confirms / what remains unknown / one next check**. Keep receipt numbers and other application details in your private records. At your [next weekly job search review](#your-weekly-job-search-review), [choose the next action](#choose-the-next-application-action) for the items that still need it.

## Interview Research Prompts

For a complete preparation path, read [Prepare for an Interview](/guides/prepare-for-an-interview/), including a fictional STAR example and a video setup check.

- Which responsibility in the posting needs clarification?
- What example shows how I solved a relevant problem?
- What would I ask about the team's workflow and feedback process?
- Which tools or working arrangements are mentioned, and which remain unclear?

After the conversation, [Follow Up After an Interview](/guides/follow-up-after-an-interview/) helps record the stated next steps, decide whether contact is appropriate, and prepare one improvement for next time.

If the next step is a task, [Prepare for a Work Sample Task](/guides/prepare-for-a-work-sample-task/) helps check the brief and submission conditions. Try the fictional exercise before handling an employer's actual material.

## Using AI to Organize Your Preparation

For a complete fictional practice pack, read [Use AI for Job Search Preparation](/guides/use-ai-for-job-search-preparation/). It includes three focused prompts and an example of unsupported claims to remove.

For a short sequence across visits, [check a rewritten summary against its source](/job-seeker-tools/guide-finder/#route-rewrite-review). Practice keeping preferences, planned checks and observed results distinct. All examples are fictional; no AI account or personal material is needed.

Try this prompt with a non-sensitive excerpt from a posting: "Separate the stated responsibilities from preferences. Identify questions the posting leaves unanswered. Do not invent requirements or add experience to my resume."

Compare the output with the original text. Keep only statements you can substantiate, and follow any employer instructions about AI use in assessments. See [AI in recruitment](/posts/the-rise-of-ai-in-recruitment/) for related reading.

These are editorial planning suggestions, not a scoring system or a promise of interviews or offers. Revisit your records weekly and verify changing details with the employer.
