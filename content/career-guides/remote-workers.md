## Start With Your Occupation

Remote work describes a work arrangement. Choose the responsibilities you want to investigate first, then narrow your search. [Indeed's remote-search guidance](https://www.indeed.com/help/job-seekers/articles/115005875163-searching-for-remote-jobs?hl=en&co=US) is a platform-specific starting point; confirm the details in each employer's posting.

## Check Each Posting

- **Location:** which country, region, or office is named?
- **Arrangement:** does the posting describe remote, hybrid, or office attendance, and does it explain what that means?
- **Schedule:** are time-zone overlap, fixed hours, or occasional travel mentioned?
- **Collaboration:** what communication, documentation, or handover expectations are stated?
- **Equipment and tools:** what does the employer explicitly provide or request?
- **Application:** where do you apply, and what evidence of relevant work is requested?

Mark missing answers as unknown. A remote filter alone does not establish that a role can be performed from any location.

## Recheck a Remote or Hybrid Arrangement

When you return to a saved posting, check the employer, role reference if shown, and listed locations before assuming both versions describe the same opening. Keep the search result's label separate from what the employer confirms for that role.

- **Work location:** Where does the employer say you can work on remote days? A named office is not an answer about where remote work is possible.
- **Attendance:** Which office or other location is used, and how often? Occasional visits and a weekly office schedule are different details to verify.
- **Shared hours:** Which hours and time zone apply? Confirm these separately from office attendance.

**Fictional recheck:** A board marks `DEMO-R09` as remote. The employer's posting for the same reference mentions office meetings but does not give a schedule. On a later check, the employer confirms one meeting per month at its North Office. Update the attendance note only. Keep remote-day location and shared hours unknown until they are confirmed. If hours matter first, ask: "What shared working hours and time zone apply to this role?"

In your own notes, keep **last confirmed detail / what changed / one open question**. At your [weekly work-arrangement review](/job-seeker-tools/#your-weekly-job-search-review), use the new information to decide what to verify or whether to prepare a draft. If the source has not changed, leave the unknowns in place.

## Keep a Verifiable Record

Record the source URL, date checked, work arrangement, and questions in the [application worksheet](/job-seeker-tools/#application-record-template). Compare [software](/career-guides/software-developers/) or [game development](/career-guides/game-developers/) responsibilities separately from the work arrangement.

## Scope and Limitations

This reviewed guide may be useful for job search research around remote work. It can be a starting point for comparing broad search channels such as general job sites with remote filters, professional networking platforms with remote filters, company career pages, and official workforce or telework research resources.

Official workforce resources can help readers understand telework and work-at-home as work arrangements. Official job-site help pages can also show how some platforms describe remote search filters, location fields, job alerts, and job posting details. Employer career pages and official job postings should still be checked directly for current work-location details, application expectations, remote-work policies, tools, and role requirements.

Remote-work expectations can vary by employer, role, location, policy, and job posting. Check official sources and employer pages for current details. Requirements, tools, job titles, remote-work policies, locations, and market conditions can change.
